The previous block stripped attention to the bone: three roles (query, key and value), a function
that compares and a weighted sum, with not a single recurrence inside. What it left alone was the
machine attention sits in, and that machine is still recurrent from top to bottom: the keys and the
values are states of a recurrent encoder, and the queries come from a recurrent decoder. Inside,
attention no longer waits for anything. Outside, every call still waits for the decoder to finish
its previous step and hand over the next query.

This lesson builds no new piece, and it's worth saying from the start what it removes and what it
keeps. It removes the recurrence, not the encoder or the decoder. The encoder still reads the source
sentence and the decoder still writes the target one, but neither of them keeps a state that passes
from one position to the next any more: each layer handles every position at once, and the only
thing that puts each token in contact with all the others is attention. Building that version takes
the rest of the block; this lesson first counts what is gained and what is lost. And it isn't a
matter of taste, because recurrence charges a price that can be counted. Take a news story of $800$
tokens and any recurrent network: reading it is $800$ steps that happen one after another, and
buying a machine with $800$ processors changes nothing, because step $401$ needs a number that step
$400$ hasn't finished computing yet. That's the first bill. The second one took the whole of Block 3
to pay: whatever position $1$ has to tell position $800$ travels through $799$ multiplications by
the same matrix, and by the time it arrives there's almost nothing left of it. The two bills have
the same source, and removing it clears both.

The diagram below is where all of this ends up: the complete architecture the block builds, Figure 1
of the paper *Attention Is All You Need*, box by box. At this point half the names mean nothing to
you, and that's fine: each box says which lesson builds it. What you can read today is the one
thing this lesson claims: turn on the marker and, of the fifteen boxes, three light up. The other

<Callout type="intuition" title="The idea in one sentence">
If attention already puts each token in contact with all the others, the recurrence is surplus.
Removing it swaps a chain of $T$ waits for a grid computed in one go and brings the path between any
two tokens down to a single weight, in exchange for more work on long texts and the loss of order.
</Callout>

## The chain of waits no computer can shorten

The recurrence from the previous block fits on one line, and that line says it all:

$$
\mathbf{h}_t = \tanh\left(\mathbf{W}_{hh}\mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{W}_{xh}\mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{b}_h\right),
$$

with $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ and $\mathbf{h}_0 = \mathbf{0}$. Of the two terms, the
second waits for nobody: $\mathbf{W}_{xh}\mathbf{x}_t$ only looks at the token in its own position,
so all $T$ products can be computed at once as soon as the text is tokenised. The first one does
wait. $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$ is the result of the previous step, and there's no way to bring it forward
without computing it. One of the two halves can be shared out between processors and the other
can't, and the one that can't is the one that fixes the result: the depth of the chain (how many
operations have to happen in a row, one after the other) is $T$.

Set that against the attention call as it was left written. The score of the pair $(i,j)$ is

$$
e_{ij} = a\left(\mathbf{q}_i, \mathbf{k}_j\right),
$$

and no $e$ from any other pair appears in it. The wait the previous block ended on, each row of
$\mathbf{Q}$ waiting for the one before, isn't in this formula: it's in the decoder that makes the
queries, which needs the context from call $i$ to produce query $i+1$. That's why the call there was
made once per step, row by row. Take that decoder away and suppose, for now, that $\mathbf{Q}$,
$\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{V}$ arrive whole, one row per position of the sentence; who makes them
without a recurrence is the question this lesson ends on. With all three in hand, each of the
$T^{2}$ scores comes from two vectors that are already there, and all $T^{2}$ can be computed at
once: that's what it means for them to fit in a single matrix product, $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}$.
The softmax normalises each row with its own entries and no row looks at another; the mixture is a
second product. Three operations in a row, and that three doesn't move when the text gets longer.

One caveat, so as not to read more into this than it says. When generating text, the decoder still
writes one token per step, because it can't look at a token it hasn't written yet. What disappears
is the wait when processing a sentence that is already complete: the source sentence always, and the
target sentence during training, when the correct translation is already known. The recurrence
couldn't take advantage of that even in training: with the translation right there, the state at
step $t$ still needed the one at $t-1$. How the decoder is trained in a single pass without being
allowed to see the answer is <Leccion slug="encoder-decoder-masking">the lesson on masks</Leccion>.

### The other distance, the one Block 3 couldn't shorten

The depth of the chain decides how long it takes. There's a second distance, and it decides
something worse.

Ask yourself which route whatever position $1$ contributes to position $T$ travels by. In the
recurrence it travels inside the state, and the state is rewritten at every step, so that
contribution goes through $T-1$ products by $\mathbf{W}_{hh}$ before it arrives. It's exactly the
count from <Leccion slug="gradiente-desvanecido">the lesson on the vanishing gradient</Leccion>:
multiplying $T-1$ times by the same matrix switches the signal off if its spectral radius is below
$1$ and blows it up if it's above, and the LSTM and the GRU didn't shorten that path; they opened an
additive path beside it so the trip could be survived.

In attention the path between those two positions is one weight:

$$
\mathbf{c}_T = \alpha_{T1}\,\mathbf{v}_1 + \alpha_{T2}\,\mathbf{v}_2 + \dots + \alpha_{TT}\,\mathbf{v}_T.
$$

What position $1$ contributes is its term, and in front of it stands a single factor: $\alpha_{T1}$.
One, not $T-1$. And since the backward pass is the forward pass travelled in reverse, the gradient
that reaches position $1$ from the loss at step $T$ doesn't go through anything in between either.
The distance between any two positions stops depending on how many lie between them, which is more
than Block 3 managed: the gate made the trip survivable, not short.

## What it costs to swap the steps for a grid

None of this is free, and the price is counted in multiplications. Start with the recurrence. A
step multiplies $\mathbf{W}_{hh} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times d_h}$ by one vector and
$\mathbf{W}_{xh} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times d_{\text{model}}}$ by another, and multiplying a matrix
by a vector costs one multiplication per entry, so the step costs one product for each entry of its
matrices. That's the count from <Leccion slug="la-rnn-vanilla">the lesson on the vanilla
RNN</Leccion>, read as a cost instead of as memory. Over the whole sentence:

$$
T \cdot \left(d_h \cdot d_h + d_h \cdot d_{\text{model}}\right) \quad \text{multiplications}.
$$

Now the grid. $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}$ multiplies a $T \times d_k$ matrix by a $d_k \times T$
one: each of the $T^{2}$ entries of the result is a dot product of $d_k$ terms, which makes
$T^{2} \cdot d_k$ multiplications. The mixture $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}$ has $T \cdot d_v$ entries and
each one sums $T$ terms, another $T^{2} \cdot d_v$. In total:

$$
T^{2} \cdot \left(d_k + d_v\right) \quad \text{multiplications}.
$$

Put pair by pair: each entry $(i,j)$ of the grid costs $d_k$ multiplications to be scored and $d_v$
to add its share to the mixture, $\alpha_{ij}\mathbf{v}_j$, and the grid has $T^{2}$ entries.

The two counts compare best with all four widths equal. Suppose they are, and call them $d$: that
leaves $2Td^{2}$ against $2T^{2}d$, and the ratio between them is $T/d$. The grid costs less than
the recurrence as long as the sentence is shorter than the model is wide, and more once it's longer;
they cross at $T = d$, and not before. In the paper these two counts are written
$O(T^{2}d)$ against $O(Td^{2})$, which is the same comparison with the constants dropped; that's how
you'll find it in its Table 1.

What this buys is worth stating in full, because it isn't "faster". It's a change of axis: the total
work grows (with $T$ squared, on long texts), and in exchange that work can be shared out, whereas
the recurrence's $T$ steps can't be shared out in any way at all. A large computation that fits in a
single matrix product finishes before a small one broken into $T$ waits.

### The price that isn't paid in multiplications

Look at the score again, and this time look closely at the indices. In $e_{ij} = a(\mathbf{q}_i,
\mathbf{k}_j)$ the subscripts only point at which two vectors go in; neither $i$ nor $j$ appears
inside the computation. The same goes for the mixture, which is a sum, and a sum doesn't distinguish
the order of its terms. The consequence is awkward: if you shuffle the rows of $\mathbf{K}$ and
$\mathbf{V}$, the outputs are shuffled the same way and not one number changes.

So <W>the dog bites the postman</W> and <W>the postman bites the dog</W> reach the layer as the same
set of vectors. The recurrence didn't have this problem, and not through any merit of its own: it
had it solved from birth, because it read in order and the reading order was the sentence's order.
Nor did the concatenated MLP from <Leccion slug="por-que-falla-el-mlp">the
lesson on why the MLP fails on sequences</Leccion>, for the opposite reason: each position multiplied
its own block of columns, which is why it could tell them apart and couldn't generalise across them.
Attention has neither.

The concession is a big one and this lesson doesn't fix it: removing the recurrence takes away the
only thing that knew the order. The fix is to add to each row something that depends on where it
is, and that's <Leccion slug="codificacion-posicional">a lesson further on, about positional
encoding</Leccion>. Until then, everything that follows treats the input as a bag of vectors.

## Check it by hand on a sentence of 50 tokens

Do both counts with numbers, which is where you see the change isn't theoretical. Take $d = 512$
(the width of the paper's model) and a sentence of $T = 50$ tokens.

The recurrence: $2 \cdot 50 \cdot 512^{2} = 26\,214\,400$ multiplications, spread over $50$ steps
that come one after another. The grid: $2 \cdot 50^{2} \cdot 512 = 2\,560\,000$, and not in $50$
waits but in two matrix products. Ten times less work, and the depth of the chain has gone from $50$
to a number that doesn't even mention $50$.

Now go up to $T = 1\,000$, a long text. The grid needs
$2 \cdot 1\,000^{2} \cdot 512 = 1\,024\,000\,000$ and the recurrence
$2 \cdot 1\,000 \cdot 512^{2} = 524\,288\,000$: almost double, and this time the expensive one is
the newcomer. The ratio between them is $1\,000/512 \approx 1.95$, exactly as $T/d$ said. Do it
yourself with $T = 2\,000$ and check that it comes out at $3.9$. And notice that the depth of the
chain still hasn't moved, while the recurrence's has gone to $2\,000$.

That's the deal, with both columns in view: you pay in work, which can be shared out between
processors, and you buy back waiting, which can't be shared out between anything.

That leaves a gap in the most visible place. The attention call needs queries, keys and values, and
throughout the previous block they were made by two recurrent networks: the queries were the
decoder's states, one per step, and the keys and the values were the encoder's. Those states have
gone with the recurrence, and without them the operation this lesson has put at the centre of the
architecture has nothing to start from.

The answer is the one that gives <Leccion slug="self-attention">the next lesson, on
self-attention</Leccion>, its name: let the sequence ask itself. If the queries, the keys and the
values are three readings of the same vectors, one per position, there's no need for a second
network to produce anything, and each position gets to look at all the others, itself included.
It's the same attention as in the previous block, with all three roles in the hands of a single
sequence, and it's what turns the diagram above into something that can be computed.
