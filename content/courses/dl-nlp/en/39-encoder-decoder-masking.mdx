The sum and the layer norm of the previous lesson, on residual connections, wrap whatever is put
inside them without asking what it is, so putting one layer on top of another is no longer the
problem. The one left isn't about depth. Everything this block has built takes for granted that the
sentence is whole and in front of it, and that the $T$ positions are computed at once, and for half
the architecture that is false: a model that translates has no translation in front of it until it
has written one. And if you train it by computing everything in one go (which is what dropping the
recurrence won), the answer ends up sitting right next to the position that has to guess it.

See it in <W>the cat drinks milk</W>, which is what the model would have to learn to write. If the
four positions are computed at once and each one looks at all of them (which is exactly what the
layer from <Leccion slug="self-attention">the lesson on self-attention</Leccion> does), the position
that has to produce <W>drinks</W> is looking at <W>milk</W> while it produces it. Guessing with the
answer in front of you goes very well: the loss falls to zero during training. When it really
generates there is nothing to the right, because it hasn't been written yet, and the model hasn't
learned to do anything else. This lesson forbids that look without giving up computing the $T$
positions at once, and along the way it builds the sublayer that joins the paper's two columns.

Go back to the self-attention grid and turn on the causal mask. It is the same sentence, with the
same projections and the same calculation; the only thing that changes is that everything to the
right of the diagonal disappears and the cells that are left share out among themselves the unit
there was to share. Start with the first row, which is the extreme case: it has no one to look at
but itself.

<Callout type="intuition" title="The idea in one sentence">
Masking is not a rule obeyed by the loop that writes: it is a $-\infty$ added to the score before
the softmax, so that row $i$ computes the same thing whether or not anything exists after it.
</Callout>

## Blanking out the future inside the softmax

The mask is a matrix the size of the grid, and its cells take only two values:

$$
M_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } j \leq i, \\ -\infty & \text{if } j > i \end{cases}.
$$

With it, the attention call changes by one term and nothing else:

$$
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \mathbf{M}\right)\mathbf{V},
$$

with $\mathbf{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$ and the softmax by rows, as always. Nothing inside is
touched: the three projections are the same, the divisor from <Leccion
slug="scaled-dot-product">the lesson on the *scaled dot-product*</Leccion> is the same, and the heads
from <Leccion slug="multi-head">the lesson on multi-head attention</Leccion> are the same (each one
receives the same $\mathbf{M}$).

Why $-\infty$ and not something else shows when you write out the whole weight:

$$
\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(e_{ij} + M_{ij}\right)}{\sum_{m=1}^{T}\exp\left(e_{im} + M_{im}\right)},
$$

where $e_{ij}$ is the score already divided by $\sqrt{d_k}$. Since $\exp(-\infty) = 0$, a blanked-out
cell disappears from the numerator *and* from its place in the denominator, both at once. That is
what leaves nothing over: the unit the softmax shares out is shared out entirely among the cells
that remain.

The two alternatives that occur to anyone fail right there. Putting a $0$ in the *score* blanks out
nothing: $\exp(0) = 1$ is a perfectly ordinary weight, and in the cell further down, row $0$ ends up
giving the future $0.525$ of its weight. And computing the softmax as usual and setting the
*weights* to zero afterwards leaves the denominator as it was, so the row sums to whatever its start
summed to ($0.085$, $0.291$, $0.871$), and what comes out is no longer a mixture but a shrunken
mixture, with a different factor in each row.

### Masking is renormalising the start of the row

There is a way of reading the mask that puts it in its place, and it comes out of the formula above
in two steps. For $j \leq i$ the term $M_{ij}$ is $0$, so the numerator is the usual one and the only
thing that changes is the denominator, which keeps the terms up to $i$. Divide top and bottom by the
full sum $\sum_{m=1}^{T}\exp(e_{im})$ and the unmasked weights appear:

$$
\alpha^{\text{causal}}_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(e_{ij}\right)}{\sum_{m \leq i}\exp\left(e_{im}\right)} = \frac{\alpha_{ij}}{\sum_{m \leq i}\alpha_{im}}, \qquad j \leq i.
$$

In other words: take the row that <Leccion slug="self-attention">self-attention</Leccion> computed,
throw away the part on the right and divide what is left by what it summed to. Three things follow
from that, and none of them needs another calculation. The order within what survives *doesn't
change*, because all those cells are divided by the same number: the mask doesn't alter
preferences, it withdraws candidates. Row $1$ is left with a single candidate and therefore with
$\alpha_{11} = 1$, whether the model knows it or not. And the mask is not a second operation beside
the softmax; it is the same softmax over fewer cells.

### One pass instead of T

We still have to say what goes into the decoder, because so far we've talked about what it can't
look at and not about what it has in front of it. What goes in is the correct output *shifted one
position*: row $i$ gets the token $y_{i-1}$, the one the model should have written at the previous
step, and row $1$ gets <W>\<GO></W>, the symbol the decoder starts from, because before the first
there is none. So row $i$ has $y_{<i}$ in front of it and has to produce $y_i$, which is precisely
the distribution the previous block wrote,

$$
P\left(y_i \mid y_{<i}, x_{1:T_x}\right),
$$

one per row. That is the *teacher forcing* of <Leccion slug="seq2seq">the lesson on
sequence-to-sequence models</Leccion>, with no change to the idea: the model is given the true token
instead of the one it wrote. What changes is the price. There, the $T_y$ rows came out of $T_y$ steps
of a recurrence; here they come out of one matrix product, and the mask is what makes that vertical
bar tell the truth: without it, row $i$ would also be conditioning on $y_{i}$, $y_{i+1}$ and
everything else.

And now the two concessions, which go together. The mask makes *training* parallel, not generation:
when it really writes there is no correct output to shift by one position, row $i$ needs the token
the previous pass produced, and that is $T_y$ passes, one per token. The other is that the triangle
saves not a single multiplication: the $T^{2}$ scores are computed in full and then more than half
of them are blanked out. The mask is a prohibition, not an optimisation.

## The sublayer that looks at the other column

The decoder has three sublayers where the encoder has two, and the middle one is the only box in the
whole paper that joins the two columns. It is the previous block's attention, whole, with the
queries read from one place and the keys and values from another:

$$
\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{dec}}\mathbf{W}^Q, \qquad \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}}\mathbf{W}^K, \qquad \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}}\mathbf{W}^V,
$$

where $\mathbf{X}^{\text{dec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_y \times d_{\text{model}}}$ is what has been written
so far and $\mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_x \times d_{\text{model}}}$ is what the
encoder left read. The superscripts on $\mathbf{X}$ say *which column* the matrix comes from; those
on $\mathbf{W}$ still say which role each one projects, as they have since <Leccion
slug="self-attention">the lesson on self-attention</Leccion>. Neither of the two counts layers.

The shape of the map falls out on its own, and it is what gives away where this sublayer comes from:

$$
\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_y \times T_x},
$$

rectangular, one row per position of the output and one column per position of the input. It is the
grid of the previous block, the one from <Leccion slug="luong">the lesson on multiplicative
attention</Leccion>, with the same two axes and the same row summing to $1$. What has changed since
then is three things, and none of them is the operation: the score is the *scaled dot-product*
instead of the alignment model, there are $h$ heads in parallel instead of one, and the query is no
longer the state of a recurrent network but a row of a matrix that exists in full.

There is no mask here, and it's worth saying why, because it sounds odd after the previous section.
What is forbidden is reading what *hasn't been written yet*, and the input is not being written: it
was read in full before starting. Position $T_x$ of the source is available from the first token of
the output, as position $1$ is. (Real implementations do put another mask here, the padding
mask, so that the filler positions of a batch don't count; it is the same mechanism applied to
another problem, and it is outside this lesson.)

With that, the paper's three calls to attention can be put in one table, and they are the three
boxes that lit up in the diagram from <Leccion slug="adios-recurrencia">the lesson that said goodbye
to recurrence</Leccion>:

| Call | Queries | Keys and values | Shape of $\mathbf{A}$ | Mask? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| encoder self-attention | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}}$ | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}}$ | $T_x \times T_x$ | no |
| masked self-attention | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{dec}}$ | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{dec}}$ | $T_y \times T_y$ | yes, causal |
| encoder-decoder attention | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{dec}}$ | $\mathbf{X}^{\text{enc}}$ | $T_y \times T_x$ | no |

One function, three sets of arguments. And the decoder's three sublayers are wrapped in the line
from the previous lesson, without a single exception: what went in is added and the result is
normalised, three times.

## The two attentions in NumPy

The first cell is the mask on its own, over a grid of scores whose numbers I set by hand so that the
sums can be followed by eye. Look at three things: row $0$, the column of sums, and what happens with
the two ways of getting it wrong.

<PyCell packages={["numpy"]} code={`
import numpy as np

E = np.array([[1.0, 2.0, 0.5, 3.0],          # scores already divided by the root of d_k
              [0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 1.0],
              [2.0, 0.0, 1.0, 0.5],
              [1.0, 1.0, 2.0, 1.5]])
T = len(E)
future = np.triu(np.ones((T, T), dtype=bool), k=1)     # True where j > i


def softmax_rows(S):
    Z = np.exp(S - S.max(axis=1, keepdims=True))
    return Z / Z.sum(axis=1, keepdims=True)


M = np.where(future, -np.inf, 0.0)
with_mask = softmax_rows(E + M)                        # the paper's
with_a_zero = softmax_rows(np.where(future, 0.0, E))   # blanking out with a 0 in the SCORE
weights_to_zero = np.where(future, 0.0, softmax_rows(E))  # blanking out the WEIGHTS, already shared out

for name, A in (("with -inf   ", with_mask),
                ("with a 0    ", with_a_zero),
                ("weights to 0", weights_to_zero)):

The first three lines are the whole argument. With $-\infty$ row $0$ comes out `[1. 0. 0. 0.]` and
all four sums are $1$. With a $0$ in the score the sums are $1$ as well, and that is the trap: the row
shares out correctly, but it shares out among positions that shouldn't be there (the $0.525$ that
goes to three columns of the future). With the weights blanked out afterwards, the sums drop to
$0.085$, $0.291$, $0.871$ and $1$: four rows scaled four different ways, which is the worst of the
three results even though it looks the most harmless.

The second cell builds the decoder's two attentions and checks the only thing the mask promises.

<PyCell packages={["numpy"]} code={`
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(8)
T_x, T_y, d_model, d_k, d_v = 7, 5, 16, 8, 8

X_enc = rng.normal(size=(T_x, d_model))       # what the encoder left read
X_dec = rng.normal(size=(T_y, d_model))       # what has been written so far
Wq = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_k)) * 0.5
Wk = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_k)) * 0.5
Wv = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_v)) * 0.5


def attention(asking, answering, causal=False):
    Q, K, V = asking @ Wq, answering @ Wk, answering @ Wv
    E = Q @ K.T / np.sqrt(d_k)
    if causal:
        E = np.where(np.triu(np.ones(E.shape, dtype=bool), k=1), -np.inf, E)
    Z = np.exp(E - E.max(axis=1, keepdims=True))
    A = Z / Z.sum(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return A @ V, A


own, A_own = attention(X_dec, X_dec, causal=True)        # masked self-attention
enc_dec, A_enc_dec = attention(X_dec, X_enc)             # encoder-decoder attention

print("masked     : A", A_own.shape, " sums", np.round(A_own.sum(axis=1), 3))

The two maps have the table's shapes ($5 \times 5$ the masked one, $5 \times 7$ the encoder-decoder
one) and all ten rows sum to $1$. Of the first one's $25$ cells, $15$ are left live, which is the
count $T(T+1)/2$. And the last two lines are the property all of this exists for: rewriting positions
$4$ and $5$ of the decoder moves what comes out at the first three by exactly $0.0$, while the fourth
(which is one of the ones I rewrote) does move. Change `causal=True` to `causal=False` and run it
again: that $0.0$ stops being one, and that is a model that has read what it hasn't written yet.
