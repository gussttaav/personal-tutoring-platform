The village stood beside the sea, and the sea came into the village every morning with the smell of salt. The houses were white and low, and the streets ran down towards the water as if the water were calling them. In the morning the fishermen went out in their boats, and in the evening they came back with their nets full, and the children ran along the beach counting the boats that came back from the sea.

The grandmother lived in the last house, the one that looked out on the harbour. She had a courtyard with a fig tree and a vine, and under the vine she laid the table when the warm weather came. There she told stories of the sea, of the ships that left and never came back, of the storms that broke the waves against the rocks, of the fish that shone like coins under the evening sun.

On market days the square filled with voices. The women bought bread and oil, fresh fish and fruit from the orchard, and the men talked about the weather and the price of fish. It smelt of warm bread and oranges, of coffee and the sea. An old man sold flowers on a corner, and a girl sold coloured shells that she had gathered on the shore.

When summer came, the village did not sleep. The nights were long and mild, and people brought their chairs out into the street to talk until very late. You could hear the music of a distant guitar, the murmur of the waves, the laughter of those coming back from the beach. The sky filled with stars, and the sea repeated them below, trembling in the dark water.

In winter everything changed. The wind came from the north and beat against the windows, and the sea turned grey and furious. The boats stayed moored in the harbour, and the fishermen mended their nets beside the fire. The grandmother lit the hearth and boiled the soup, and the smell of the soup filled the whole house. Outside it rained without stopping, and inside it was warm and good.

The boy wanted to be a fisherman, like his father and like the father of his father. He learnt to row before he learnt to write, and he knew the name of every boat and every rock on the coast. He could read the sky: when the clouds came low and fast there would be a storm, and when the sea lay smooth as a mirror the day would be good for going out to fish.

The years went by, and the village stayed beside the sea, white and low, with its streets running down towards the water. The children who ran along the beach became fishermen, and other children ran later along the same beach. The grandmother was gone, but her house still looked out on the harbour, and under the vine someone still laid the table when the warm weather came. The sea came in every morning with the smell of salt, and every evening the boats came back to the harbour with their nets full of fish that shone like coins under the sun.
